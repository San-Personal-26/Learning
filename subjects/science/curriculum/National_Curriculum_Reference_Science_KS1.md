# National Curriculum Reference — Key Stage 1 Science

Source: DfE "National curriculum in England: science programmes of study" — Key Stage 1 subject content (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study).

Structured the same way the maths reference (`CURRICULUM_REFERENCE.md`) was — lettered main sections, numbered subsections — so each can be used as a short code, e.g. `B.2`, for lesson content to cite per CLAUDE.md's "cite the National Curriculum objective" rule.

**Important caveat:** the National Curriculum publishes Key Stage 1 (Years 1–2) science as a single combined programme of study — it does not split statements out by individual year group the way Key Stage 2 does. The table below is the complete KS1 content, in the document's original order. Deciding which of these map to Year 1 specifically (vs. Year 2) is a sequencing decision for this project to make, not something the source document specifies.

**"Working scientifically" note:** the source document is explicit that these are not a standalone unit to be taught in isolation — they describe the practical methods, processes and skills to be taught *through* the content sections below (Plants, Animals, Materials, Seasonal changes), across both years of the key stage.

**Sub-skill breakdowns** under each table are draft v1 — a further, lesson-sized decomposition of each broad NC statement, written independently for this project (not sourced from any third-party scheme). They're a starting point to refine as actual lesson content gets built, not a final spec. Refs follow the pattern `B.2.1` (strand B, NC statement 2, sub-skill 1).

## A. Working Scientifically

| Ref | Statement |
|---|---|
| A.1 | Ask simple questions and recognise that they can be answered in different ways |
| A.2 | Observe closely, using simple equipment |
| A.3 | Perform simple tests |
| A.4 | Identify and classify |
| A.5 | Use their observations and ideas to suggest answers to questions |
| A.6 | Gather and record data to help in answering questions |

### Sub-skill breakdown (draft v1)

- **A.1** — A.1.1 Generate a simple question about an everyday object or phenomenon · A.1.2 Recognise that a question could be answered by observing, testing, or asking/looking it up
- **A.2** — A.2.1 Use the senses to make close observations · A.2.2 Use simple equipment (hand lens, ruler, thermometer) to support an observation
- **A.3** — A.3.1 Carry out a simple test with adult guidance · A.3.2 Follow a short set of instructions safely to complete a test
- **A.4** — A.4.1 Sort objects or living things into groups using an observable feature · A.4.2 Decide what something is based on given criteria
- **A.5** — A.5.1 Use an observation to suggest a possible answer to a question · A.5.2 Talk about or explain what was found out
- **A.6** — A.6.1 Record simple observations in a picture, tally or table · A.6.2 Use recorded data to help answer the original question

## B. Plants

| Ref | Statement |
|---|---|
| B.1 | Identify and name a variety of common wild and garden plants, including deciduous and evergreen trees |
| B.2 | Identify and describe the basic structure of a variety of common flowering plants, including trees |

### Sub-skill breakdown (draft v1)

- **B.1** — B.1.1 Name common garden plants · B.1.2 Name common wild plants · B.1.3 Distinguish deciduous from evergreen trees
- **B.2** — B.2.1 Identify the basic parts of a flowering plant (roots, stem, leaves, flower) · B.2.2 Identify the basic parts of a tree (roots, trunk, branches, leaves) · B.2.3 Label a simple diagram of a plant or tree

## C. Animals, Including Humans

| Ref | Statement |
|---|---|
| C.1 | Identify and name a variety of common animals including fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals |
| C.2 | Identify and name a variety of common animals that are carnivores, herbivores and omnivores |
| C.3 | Describe and compare the structure of a variety of common animals (fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals, including pets) |
| C.4 | Identify, name, draw and label the basic parts of the human body and say which part of the body is associated with each sense |

### Sub-skill breakdown (draft v1)

- **C.1** — C.1.1 Sort animals into the five vertebrate groups · C.1.2 Name familiar examples within each group
- **C.2** — C.2.1 Define carnivore, herbivore and omnivore · C.2.2 Classify a given animal by what it eats
- **C.3** — C.3.1 Compare body coverings (scales, feathers, fur) · C.3.2 Compare how different animals move · C.3.3 Compare a pet's features to those of a wild animal in the same group
- **C.4** — C.4.1 Label major external body parts · C.4.2 Match each of the five senses to its body part · C.4.3 Draw and label a simple body diagram

## D. Everyday Materials

| Ref | Statement |
|---|---|
| D.1 | Distinguish between an object and the material from which it is made |
| D.2 | Identify and name a variety of everyday materials, including wood, plastic, glass, metal, water, and rock |
| D.3 | Describe the simple physical properties of a variety of everyday materials |
| D.4 | Compare and group together a variety of everyday materials on the basis of their simple physical properties |

### Sub-skill breakdown (draft v1)

- **D.1** — D.1.1 Distinguish "what it's made of" from "what it is" for everyday objects
- **D.2** — D.2.1 Name common materials · D.2.2 Identify the material an object is made from · D.2.3 Find examples of a named material around the room
- **D.3** — D.3.1 Describe hard/soft, stretchy/stiff, shiny/dull, rough/smooth, bendy/not bendy, waterproof/absorbent, transparent/opaque
- **D.4** — D.4.1 Sort materials by a single property · D.4.2 Sort materials by more than one property at once · D.4.3 Explain why a material suits a particular use (e.g. glass for windows, wool for a jumper)

## E. Seasonal Changes

| Ref | Statement |
|---|---|
| E.1 | Observe changes across the four seasons |
| E.2 | Observe and describe weather associated with the seasons and how day length varies |

### Sub-skill breakdown (draft v1)

- **E.1** — E.1.1 Name the four seasons in order · E.1.2 Describe how trees and plants change across the seasons · E.1.3 Keep a simple seasonal observation log
- **E.2** — E.2.1 Describe typical weather in each season · E.2.2 Compare day length across the seasons · E.2.3 Record weather using simple symbols or a chart
