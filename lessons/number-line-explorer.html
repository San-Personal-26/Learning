Explore: which way is bigger?

What does a number line tell us?

Moving right
Numbers get bigger as you move right along a number line — 5 is to the right of 2, so 5 is bigger.
Moving left
Numbers get smaller as you move left — and they can keep going past zero into negative numbers. −5 is to the left of −2, so −5 is the smaller (colder, lower, more overdrawn) number.
Comparing symbols
We write comparisons using =, ≠, <, >, ≤, ≥ — the pointy end of < and > always points at the smaller number, like a hungry arrow aiming at the smaller snack.

🌡️ Below zero

A thermometer showing −5°C isn't "5 gone wrong" — it's five degrees colder than freezing. Same idea for a bank balance (−£20 means you owe £20) or a lift (floor −1 is one level below ground). Negative numbers describe real things.

Does more digits mean bigger?

Which is longer, 0.6 m or 0.58 m? It's tempting to compare digit-by-digit without lining things up — the trick is to line up the decimal points, padding with zeros if needed.

0️⃣ Padding with zeros

0.9 and 0.90 are exactly the same number — adding a zero on the end of a decimal never changes its value. So you can always pad the shorter number with zeros until both have the same number of places, then compare normally: 0.90 vs 0.85 — easy, 0.90 wins.

Which is bigger?

Which is more pizza, 3/4 or 5/8? Fractions with different denominators can't be compared directly — convert to a common denominator, or use a quick benchmark like 1/2.

⚖️ Benchmark fractions

Fast sanity check: is it bigger or smaller than 1/2? 3/4 is clearly more than half a pizza; 5/8 is only a little more than half. That alone tells you 3/4 wins — no common denominator needed.

Recap

  • ✓A number line puts smaller numbers to the left and bigger numbers to the right — including negative numbers, which sit left of zero.
  • ✓More digits after the decimal point doesn't mean a bigger number — line up the place values (padding with zeros helps) to compare properly.
  • ✓To compare fractions with different denominators, convert to a common denominator, or compare each to a benchmark like 1/2.
  • ✓=, ≠, <, >, ≤, ≥ are shorthand for "the same as", "different from", "smaller than", "bigger than" — and their "...or equal to" versions.
