# National Curriculum Reference — Key Stage 3 Mathematics

Source: DfE "National curriculum in England: mathematics programmes of study" — Key Stage 3 subject content (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study).

Structured the same way the IXL reference page was (lettered main sections, numbered subsections), so each can be used as a short code — e.g. `A.3` — for lesson content to cite per CLAUDE.md's "cite the National Curriculum objective" rule.

**Important caveat:** the National Curriculum publishes Key Stage 3 (Years 7–9) as a single combined programme of study — it does not split statements out by individual year group. The table below is the complete KS3 content, in the document's original order. Deciding which of these map to Year 8 specifically (vs. Year 7 or Year 9) is a sequencing decision for this project to make, not something the source document specifies.

**Sub-skill breakdowns** under each table are draft v1 — a further, lesson-sized decomposition of each broad NC statement, written independently for this project (not sourced from any third-party scheme). They're a starting point to refine as actual lesson content gets built, not a final spec. Refs follow the pattern `A.3.2` (strand A, NC statement 3, sub-skill 2).

## A. Number

| Ref | Statement |
|---|---|
| A.1 | Understand and use place value for decimals, measures and integers of any size |
| A.2 | Order positive and negative integers, decimals and fractions; use the number line as a model for ordering of the real numbers; use the symbols =, ≠, <, >, ≤, ≥ |
| A.3 | Use the concepts and vocabulary of prime numbers, factors (or divisors), multiples, common factors, common multiples, highest common factor, lowest common multiple, prime factorisation, including using product notation and the unique factorisation property |
| A.4 | Use the 4 operations, including formal written methods, applied to integers, decimals, proper and improper fractions, and mixed numbers, all both positive and negative |
| A.5 | Use conventional notation for the priority of operations, including brackets, powers, roots and reciprocals |
| A.6 | Recognise and use relationships between operations including inverse operations |
| A.7 | Use integer powers and associated real roots (square, cube and higher), recognise powers of 2, 3, 4, 5 and distinguish between exact representations of roots and their decimal approximations |
| A.8 | Interpret and compare numbers in standard form A × 10ⁿ, 1≤A<10, where n is a positive or negative integer or 0 |
| A.9 | Work interchangeably with terminating decimals and their corresponding fractions (such as 3.5 and 7/2 or 0.375 and 3/8) |
| A.10 | Define percentage as "number of parts per hundred", interpret percentages and percentage changes as a fraction or a decimal, interpret these multiplicatively, express 1 quantity as a percentage of another, compare 2 quantities using percentages, and work with percentages greater than 100% |
| A.11 | Interpret fractions and percentages as operators |
| A.12 | Use standard units of mass, length, time, money and other measures, including with decimal quantities |
| A.13 | Round numbers and measures to an appropriate degree of accuracy (e.g. to a number of decimal places or significant figures) |
| A.14 | Use approximation through rounding to estimate answers and calculate possible resulting errors expressed using inequality notation a<x≤b |
| A.15 | Use a calculator and other technologies to calculate results accurately and then interpret them appropriately |
| A.16 | Appreciate the infinite nature of the sets of integers, real and rational numbers |

- **A.5** — A.5.1 Apply BIDMAS to numerical expressions · A.5.2 Evaluate expressions with brackets and powers · A.5.3 Evaluate expressions with roots and reciprocals
- **A.6** — A.6.1 Use inverse operations to check answers · A.6.2 Use inverse operations to solve missing-number problems
- **A.7** — A.7.1 Calculate squares and cubes · A.7.2 Find square/cube roots of perfect squares/cubes · A.7.3 Estimate roots of non-perfect squares · A.7.4 Distinguish exact roots from decimal approximations
- **A.8** — A.8.1 Convert large numbers to standard form · A.8.2 Convert small decimals to standard form · A.8.3 Convert standard form back to ordinary numbers · A.8.4 Compare numbers in standard form
- **A.9** — A.9.1 Convert terminating decimals to fractions · A.9.2 Convert fractions to terminating decimals · A.9.3 Spot equivalent decimal/fraction pairs
- **A.10** — A.10.1 Convert between percentages, fractions, decimals · A.10.2 Find a percentage of an amount · A.10.3 Express one quantity as a percentage of another · A.10.4 Percentage increase/decrease · A.10.5 Work with percentages over 100%
- **A.11** — A.11.1 Apply a fraction operator (e.g. find ¾ of a quantity) · A.11.2 Apply a percentage operator in real contexts (discounts, VAT)
- **A.12** — A.12.1 Convert metric units (length, mass, capacity) · A.12.2 Calculate with money · A.12.3 Calculate with time (24-hour clock, durations)
- **A.13** — A.13.1 Round to a given number of decimal places · A.13.2 Round to a given number of significant figures · A.13.3 Choose sensible accuracy for a real-world context
- **A.14** — A.14.1 Estimate answers via rounding · A.14.2 Express bounds of a rounded/measured value using inequality notation
- **A.15** — A.15.1 Multi-step calculations on a calculator · A.15.2 Interpret calculator output sensibly (e.g. rounding money to 2dp)
- **A.16** — A.16.1 There's no largest integer (conceptual/exploratory) · A.16.2 Rational numbers are dense on the number line (conceptual/exploratory) — note: more suited to a short discussion/enrichment piece than a full interactive lesson

## B. Algebra

| Ref | Statement |
|---|---|
| B.1 | Use and interpret algebraic notation, including: ab in place of a × b; 3y in place of y + y + y and 3 × y; a² in place of a × a, a³ in place of a × a × a; a²b in place of a × a × b; a/b in place of a ÷ b; coefficients written as fractions rather than as decimals; brackets |
| B.2 | Substitute numerical values into formulae and expressions, including scientific formulae |
| B.3 | Understand and use the concepts and vocabulary of expressions, equations, inequalities, terms and factors |
| B.4 | Simplify and manipulate algebraic expressions to maintain equivalence by: collecting like terms; multiplying a single term over a bracket; taking out common factors; expanding products of 2 or more binomials |
| B.5 | Understand and use standard mathematical formulae; rearrange formulae to change the subject |
| B.6 | Model situations or procedures by translating them into algebraic expressions or formulae and by using graphs |
| B.7 | Use algebraic methods to solve linear equations in 1 variable (including all forms that require rearrangement) |
| B.8 | Work with coordinates in all 4 quadrants |
| B.9 | Recognise, sketch and produce graphs of linear and quadratic functions of 1 variable with appropriate scaling, using equations in x and y and the Cartesian plane |
| B.10 | Interpret mathematical relationships both algebraically and graphically |
| B.11 | Reduce a given linear equation in 2 variables to the standard form y = mx + c; calculate and interpret gradients and intercepts of graphs of such linear equations numerically, graphically and algebraically |
| B.12 | Use linear and quadratic graphs to estimate values of y for given values of x and vice versa and to find approximate solutions of simultaneous linear equations |
| B.13 | Find approximate solutions to contextual problems from given graphs of a variety of functions, including piece-wise linear, exponential and reciprocal graphs |
| B.14 | Generate terms of a sequence from either a term-to-term or a position-to-term rule |
| B.15 | Recognise arithmetic sequences and find the nth term |
| B.16 | Recognise geometric sequences and appreciate other sequences that arise |

### Sub-skill breakdown (draft v1)

- **B.1** — B.1.1 Interpret/write expressions using implied multiplication (ab, 3y) · B.1.2 Interpret/write expressions using powers (a², a³) · B.1.3 Interpret expressions with combined terms (a²b) · B.1.4 Interpret algebraic fractions and fractional coefficients · B.1.5 Interpret expressions involving brackets
- **B.2** — B.2.1 Substitute into simple expressions · B.2.2 Substitute into multi-step formulae · B.2.3 Substitute into real-life/scientific formulae (speed, area, conversions)
- **B.3** — B.3.1 Identify terms, coefficients and factors in an expression · B.3.2 Distinguish expressions, equations and inequalities · B.3.3 Use correct algebraic vocabulary
- **B.4** — B.4.1 Collect like terms · B.4.2 Expand a single term over a bracket · B.4.3 Take out common factors (simple factorising) · B.4.4 Expand products of two binomials
- **B.5** — B.5.1 Use standard formulae (area, perimeter, speed-distance-time) · B.5.2 Rearrange a formula to change the subject
- **B.6** — B.6.1 Translate a real-life situation into an expression/formula · B.6.2 Translate a real-life situation into a graph
- **B.7** — B.7.1 Solve one-step equations · B.7.2 Solve two-step equations · B.7.3 Solve equations with the unknown on both sides · B.7.4 Solve equations involving brackets · B.7.5 Real-life word problems using linear equations
- **B.8** — B.8.1 Plot/read coordinates in all four quadrants · B.8.2 Find midpoints and simple distances between coordinates (introductory)
- **B.9** — B.9.1 Plot a linear graph from a table of values · B.9.2 Recognise the shape of a quadratic graph · B.9.3 Plot a simple quadratic graph from a table of values
- **B.10** — B.10.1 Match an equation to its graph · B.10.2 Interpret a graph in a real-life context (e.g. distance–time)
- **B.11** — B.11.1 Identify gradient and y-intercept from y = mx + c · B.11.2 Calculate gradient from a graph · B.11.3 Write the equation of a line from its graph
- **B.12** — B.12.1 Read values of y for given x (and vice versa) from a graph · B.12.2 Find the approximate solution of two linear equations from where their graphs cross
- **B.13** — B.13.1 Interpret real-life graphs with varying/piecewise sections (e.g. a journey graph) · B.13.2 Recognise growth/decay-shaped real-life graphs (introductory)
- **B.14** — B.14.1 Generate a sequence from a term-to-term rule · B.14.2 Generate a sequence from a position-to-term rule
- **B.15** — B.15.1 Recognise an arithmetic sequence and find the common difference · B.15.2 Find the nth term of an arithmetic sequence · B.15.3 Use the nth term to find a term or check membership
- **B.16** — B.16.1 Recognise a geometric sequence and find the common ratio · B.16.2 Generate terms of a geometric sequence · B.16.3 Explore other sequence types (e.g. triangular numbers)
